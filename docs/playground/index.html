Inky Almanac playground

The Inky Almanac is an 800×480 poster for a seven-colour e-ink panel: a landscape photo beside the coming hour's weather, ranked by how unusual it is, with hazard warnings when there are any. Move the controls to see how the layout and the rules respond: which fact leads, when the chart shrinks, when the panel would bother to redraw, and when the rain radar counts as rain. The buttons on the frame work too.

This page is a JavaScript approximation for exploring. The Python renderer is the source of truth, and the frame's behaviour is explained in behaviours.md (the reasons are in design.md). Everything here is made up for central Sydney: the photo, the warning map and the radar are drawn by this page. Nothing is fetched apart from web fonts.

Would this redraw?

Between the hourly redraws the frame re-checks a remarkable hour sooner, and redraws only if what it shows changed enough since it was last drawn (how). Mark a state as drawn, change something, and see.

How close is this to the real poster?

  • Same rules, ported by hand: the fact scoring and its constants, the header's lead rule, the forecast line, the hero, the facts table, the chart's labels, the bottom-up layout and the badges follow server/almanac/render.py and facts.py line by line. The Python is the source of truth: if they disagree, this page is wrong.
  • Fonts: the renderer uses DejaVu Sans (Condensed). This page uses DejaVu if it's installed, otherwise Roboto stand-ins, so line breaks and widths can differ by a few pixels. The fit badge measures this page's text, not the renderer's.
  • Pixels: the canvas is drawn with anti-aliasing and then snapped back to the seven inks, so edges are close to, not identical with, Pillow's.
  • The photo: a synthetic landscape in smooth bands with a light ordered dither. Real photos are Wikimedia Commons pictures dithered with Atkinson diffusion, which looks grainier.
  • The warning map and the radar are synthetic. No Bureau of Meteorology imagery is used: BOM's feeds are licensed for personal, non-commercial use. The radar's rain colours, stipple, home ring and the "rain within 64 km" test (level 2 and up, clusters of at least 160 pixels) are the renderer's; the coastline is invented.
  • The weather: the controls build an hourly forecast the way the renderer's test fixtures do (server/tests/panel_fixtures.py), then anchor it to the BOM-style max and min. Normals are the committed central-Sydney placeholder for temperature and ARPANSA's measured Sydney UV, as the renderer uses before it has built ERA5 normals. Sunrise, sunset and the moon are calculated for central Sydney.
  • Left out: the observed temperature in the hero, the stale-data badge, radar-mode exits and the alert id. The redraw panel shows the fact-score rules only.
